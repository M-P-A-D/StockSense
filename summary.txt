StockSense Project Summary
=========================

1. What this project is
-----------------------
StockSense is an analytics and decision-support project for stock market analysis.
It is mainly an AI/ML-inspired data analysis project rather than a fully trained machine learning system.

Why:
- It uses historical and live market data.
- It computes technical indicators and summaries.
- It generates rule-based signals and risk scores.
- It does not train a model on past data to predict prices with high certainty.

So the project is best described as:
- Data analysis project
- Analytics dashboard project
- Rule-based decision support system
- AI/ML-inspired, but not a full AI/ML model in the traditional sense

2. What problem it solves
-------------------------
The project helps users quickly understand a stock by combining:
- price movement
- technical indicators
- risk level
- company fundamentals
- news and financial context

It gives a simple, explainable overview of whether a stock looks risky, bullish, bearish, or neutral.
It is useful for education, exploration, and decision support, not for guaranteed investment advice.

3. How the project works
------------------------
The app takes a stock ticker or company name, fetches data from Yahoo Finance, then computes several indicators and displays them in a Streamlit dashboard.

Main workflow:
1. User enters a stock name or ticker.
2. The app resolves the ticker using Yahoo Finance data.
3. Historical price data is fetched.
4. Technical indicators and risk metrics are calculated.
5. A rule-based buy/sell signal and a risk index are generated.
6. The results are shown in charts, tables, and summaries.

4. Formula explanations and justification
---------------------------------------
The project uses several well-known technical analysis formulas.
These are justified because they are common, transparent, and widely used in market analysis.

A) Relative Strength Index (RSI)
- Formula: RSI is based on the average gains and average losses over a given period.
- Purpose: Measures whether a stock is becoming overbought or oversold.
- Justification: RSI is a standard momentum indicator used to detect strength or weakness.
- Interpretation:
  - RSI above 70: often considered overbought
  - RSI below 30: often considered oversold

B) Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD)
- Formula: MACD is calculated by subtracting a slower moving average from a faster moving average.
- Purpose: Measures momentum and trend direction.
- Justification: MACD is widely used to identify momentum changes and trend shifts.
- Interpretation:
  - MACD line above signal line: bullish momentum
  - MACD line below signal line: bearish momentum

C) Bollinger Bands
- Formula: Upper and lower bands are calculated around a moving average using standard deviation.
- Purpose: Shows volatility and possible price extremes.
- Justification: Bollinger Bands are commonly used to identify volatility and reversals.
- Interpretation:
  - Price near upper band: possible overbought condition
  - Price near lower band: possible oversold condition

D) Exponential Moving Average (EMA) and Simple Moving Average (SMA)
- Formula: EMA gives more weight to recent prices; SMA gives equal weight over a period.
- Purpose: Helps identify trend direction.
- Justification: Moving averages are basic trend-following tools and are widely accepted in technical analysis.

E) Average True Range (ATR)
- Formula: ATR measures the average range of price movement over a period.
- Purpose: Shows volatility.
- Justification: ATR is a standard tool for understanding how much price varies.

F) VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)
- Formula: Weighted by volume over time.
- Purpose: Shows the average price traded with volume weighting.
- Justification: VWAP is commonly used to understand average transaction price in relation to volume.

G) Stochastic Oscillator
- Formula: Compares current price to its recent range.
- Purpose: Detects momentum and possible overbought or oversold conditions.
- Justification: This is a standard momentum indicator used in technical analysis.

5. How the Risk Index works
---------------------------
The Risk Index is a composite score from 0 to 100.
A higher score means higher perceived risk.

The current version combines four factors:
- Annualized volatility
- Maximum drawdown
- RSI level
- Volume spike ratio

The formula uses weighted contributions:
- Volatility contributes 40%
- Drawdown contributes 30%
- RSI contributes 20%
- Volume spike contributes 10%

Why this is reasonable:
- Volatility reflects how much the stock moves.
- Drawdown reflects how badly the stock can fall from a peak.
- RSI captures overbought/oversold momentum conditions.
- Volume spike helps flag unusual trading activity.

Interpretation:
- 0 to 35: relatively lower risk
- 35 to 70: moderate risk
- 70 to 100: higher risk

Important note:
The Risk Index is not a true financial risk model. It is a rule-based educational score built from a few indicators.

6. Confidence Levels
--------------------
The confidence shown for the outlook is based on how many signals agree.
The project checks:
- the recent trend direction
- the MACD signal
- the RSI position

The confidence is then converted into a simple percentage.

Meaning:
- High confidence means several indicators are pointing in the same direction.
- Low confidence means the signals are mixed or weak.

Important note:
Confidence here is not a probability guarantee. It is a heuristic score used to show how strong the current evidence appears.

7. Buy/Sell signal explanation
------------------------------
The buy/sell signal is also rule-based.
It uses conditions such as:
- price above 50-day and 200-day moving averages
- MACD bullish or bearish
- RSI overbought or oversold
- price near upper or lower Bollinger Band
- overall risk level

The system adds or subtracts points based on these conditions and then produces one of three outputs:
- BUY
- HOLD / WATCH
- DON'T BUY

This is not an investment recommendation. It is an educational signal.

8. Is everything ethical?
-------------------------
Mostly yes, but with important limits.

Ethical aspects:
- The project uses public market data.
- It aims to educate users and support analysis.
- It is transparent about being rule-based and not a guarantee.
- It avoids pretending to be an authoritative financial advisor.

Potential concerns:
- Financial data can influence user decisions.
- The project could be misused by someone relying on it for real trading without professional advice.
- The outputs are simplified and may miss important real-world factors.

Overall ethical view:
The project is ethical when used for learning, research, and education.
It becomes less ethical when used as a substitute for professional financial advice or as a basis for major financial decisions.

9. Overall conclusion
---------------------
This project is a practical analytics dashboard that combines financial data, technical indicators, and explainable rules to help users understand stock behavior.
It is not a fully trained AI model and should not be treated as a guaranteed prediction tool.
It is best used as an educational and analytical support tool.
